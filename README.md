# PdM at Scale

Experiments for the paper "Agentic AI Workflows for Predictive Maintenance at Portfolio Scale" (IFAC SAFEPROCESS).

The goal is to screen product portfolios for potential and meaningful candidates for predictive maintenance (PdM) with LLMs.
A product is a potential and meaningful PdM candidate if, in at least one realistic use, it wears out or degrades in a way which shows measurable signs early enough to predict an upcoming failure.

## Data availability

Keep these files in your local checkout.

## Experiment: WAGO negative, SMC positive

We use the WAGO sample as the negative case (passive terminal blocks and jumpers, no PdM candidates) and the SMC sample as the positive case (pneumatic cylinders, grippers and ionizers).
To measure how well the pipelines tell them apart, we first label the SMC products by hand.

### Ground truth of SMC

The labels are in `data/smc/ground_truth.json`, generated by `pdm_at_scale/label_smc.py`. This synthetic row illustrates the schema:

```json
{
  "product_id": "EXAMPLE-001",
  "product_designation": "Example pneumatic gripper",
  "gt_label": true,
  "gt_ordinal": "HIGH",
  "gt_explanation": "Example rationale: seal and finger guide wear show early as leakage, slower opening and closing, and a lower gripping force.",
  "gt_ordinal_score": 1.0
}
```

* `gt_label` is the binary label (is a PdM candidate or not).
* `gt_ordinal` is the ordinal label as in the paper: `NONE`, `LOW`, `MODERATE` or `HIGH`.
  `MODERATE` and `HIGH` are positive, `NONE` and `LOW` negative; the data model enforces this consistency.
* `gt_ordinal_score` maps the ordinal label to [0, 1] in equal steps (0, 1/3, 2/3, 1) for comparisons on a numeric scale.
* `gt_explanation` is the free-text explanation as in the qualitative assessment of the paper: whether and why the product is suitable for PdM.

#### Target

We follow the target of the paper: a product is a potential and meaningful PdM candidate if, in at least one realistic use, it wears out or degrades in a way which shows measurable signs early enough to predict an upcoming failure.
The OEM does not know the application at the time of the screening, so the following are deliberately **not** part of the target:

* the criticality and the downtime costs,
* the price of the product (e.g., "cheap, replaced on failure"),
* whether a sensor is already built in (the paper assumes system-level measurements are available).

The mere possibility of a failure is not enough, as almost any product would then qualify.

#### Rubric

* `HIGH`: a progressive degradation is the dominant failure mode, its precursors are measurable with common sensing, and they appear early enough to plan the maintenance before the functional failure.
* `MODERATE`: a plausible progressive degradation with measurable precursors, but the lead time is uncertain or the maintenance is mostly condition-based servicing (e.g., cleaning) rather than preventing a failure.
* `LOW`: a degradation mechanism exists, but its precursors are hardly measurable or appear too late to act on.
* `NONE`: no credible measurable precursor of the failure modes beyond the generic ageing of any product.

#### Labels

We label the product series, not the individual products, since all the products of a series share the same degradation mechanisms; size, stroke and price do not change them.
The rules, with their rationale, are in `RULES` in `pdm_at_scale/label_smc.py`; the first matching rule wins, and a product without a matching rule is an error.

| Series | Count | Ordinal | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Cylinders CQM, CU, CUJ, CUW, RQ, MGP | 113 | HIGH | Seal (and guide, return spring) wear shows as rising leakage, longer stroke times and higher air consumption well before the cylinder stalls. |
| Air grippers MH* | 53 | HIGH | Seal and finger guide wear show as rising leakage, slower motion and a falling gripping force before the gripper fails to hold the workpiece. |
| Ionizers IZS40-42, IZT40-42 | 32 | MODERATE | Emitter contamination and erosion show as a drifting ion balance and a longer discharge time, but the action is mostly condition-based cleaning. |
| Electrostatic sensor monitors IZE11x | 2 | NONE | Display and switching electronics; nothing beyond generic ageing. |

To review the labels, edit the rules and re-generate the file rather than editing the JSON by hand, so that the labels stay reproducible.

#### Caveats for the evaluation

* SMC alone is 99% positive, so binary accuracy on SMC is meaningless; evaluate on WAGO (negative) and SMC (positive) together and report precision and recall, or compare the ordinal scores.
* The explanations are per series, so they are not 200 independent assessments.
* The labels come from the authors' assessment of the product type, not from field data. Validate them against the expert labels of the SMC business developer for the 20 products he reviewed.
* The source data has an inconsistency: the CUJ products ending in `S`/`SM` (single-acting in SMC's order code) are partly designated as "Double-acting". The rationale of CUJ covers both variants.

## Experiment: XGBoost ablation

As a supervised baseline, we train XGBoost on BAAI embeddings (bge-large-en-v1.5) of the product ID and designation, i.e., what the pipelines read: a binary classifier and a regressor of the ordinal score.
The SMC products get the labels of `data/smc/ground_truth.json`; the WAGO products are negative and `LOW`, since they degrade, e.g., at the contacts, but without measurable signs.

The SMC labels are assigned per family, and the products of a family have nearly the same text, so a random split lets the classifier score perfectly by recognizing the family.
We therefore split whole families into five folds (SMC by labelling rule, WAGO by `ManufacturerProductFamily`), train on four folds and test on the held-out one.

```
venv/bin/pip install -e '.[dev,ablation]'
venv/bin/pdm-at-scale-ablation-dataset
venv/bin/pdm-at-scale-ablation-embed
venv/bin/pdm-at-scale-ablation-split --by_family
venv/bin/pdm-at-scale-ablation-train
venv/bin/pdm-at-scale-ablation-evaluate --pipelines data/pipelines/pipelines.json
```

The evaluation is written to `results/xgboost_ablation/family_seed0/evaluation`.
The public [run settings](results/xgboost_ablation/family_seed0/evaluation/run_settings.json)
come from the paper run (XGBoost 3.4.1). Its held-out predictions covered
500 products in 98 families across five folds, with 87--115 products and
3--26 families per fold. Each family was assigned to a single fold.
Request the product-level out-of-fold predictions and the complete run
metadata from the corresponding author.
With data access, place the three prediction CSVs named in
`data/pipelines/pipelines.json` beside that file. The evaluator compares WAGO and
SMC predictions with the author-assigned reference labels. Omitting
`--pipelines` scores only XGBoost.
For the random split (50/25/25), run `split` without `--by_family`, then `train --split data/xgboost_ablation/split_random_seed0.csv` and `evaluate --run_dir results/xgboost_ablation/random_seed0`.

## Reproduce the paper's pipeline figures

Run these commands from the repository root with the virtual environment
from Setup:

```sh
venv/bin/pip install -e '.[figures]'
venv/bin/python scripts/figures/plot_positive_recommendations.py
venv/bin/python scripts/figures/plot_binary_disagreement.py
```

Both scripts read aggregate counts in `results/pipelines/` and generate
PDF, SVG, and PNG files in `results/pipelines/figures/`. They need no
product records or model calls. Positive recommendations count the
pipelines' binary `TRUE` outputs. Pairwise disagreement counts products
with different binary labels. Use `--help` for font size, figure dimensions,
and output options.

## Generate screening predictions

The XGBoost evaluation above reads the saved predictions once you have
placed them in `data/pipelines/`.

To generate new predictions, use Python 3.10+ and an authenticated OpenAI
Codex CLI on your PATH, or set `CODEX_CLI_PATH` to its executable. The runners
default to `gpt-5.6-sol` with low reasoning effort. Running them requires
access to that model and uses model quota. From the repository root:

```sh
python3 scripts/pipelines/run_pipeline1.py --output-dir runs/pipeline1
python3 scripts/pipelines/run_pipelines.py --output-dir runs/pipelines2_3
```

Use a new output directory for each command. Results, prompts, logs, and
progress are written there. Pipeline 1 uses nine sessions. The combined
Pipeline 2 and 3 runner uses 27 sessions. Both read the exact 600 product IDs
and designations in `data/pipelines/inputs/` and the verbatim screening question
in `scripts/pipelines/question.txt`.

To run only Pipeline 3, use the standalone runner:

```sh
python3 scripts/pipelines/run_pipeline3_system_context.py --output-dir runs/pipeline3
```

The standalone runner also supports `--resume`. Its options are listed under
`--help`. New model runs can produce different recommendations.

### Export predictions for evaluation

After the runs finish, combine their per-manufacturer outputs into one CSV
per pipeline. This step reads the saved outputs and makes no model calls.

```sh
python3 scripts/pipelines/export_predictions.py \
  --pipeline1-dir runs/pipeline1 \
  --pipelines23-dir runs/pipelines2_3
```

The script checks the outputs against the local input CSVs and writes the
three files named in `data/pipelines/pipelines.json` beside that file. Git
ignores these product-level exports in the default location. To use a
standalone Pipeline 3 run, add `--pipeline3-dir runs/pipeline3`. If exports
already exist, use a fresh `--output-dir` inside the ignored `runs/` folder
and place a copy of `pipelines.json` beside those CSVs before passing its
path to the evaluator's `--pipelines` option.
